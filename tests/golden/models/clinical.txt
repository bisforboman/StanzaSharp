History of present illness: The patient is a 67-year-old man with a history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic kidney disease who presented to the emergency department with three days of worsening shortness of breath and a productive cough. He denies chest pain, fever or recent travel. He reports mild swelling of both ankles since last week.

Past medical history: Coronary artery disease status post stent placement in 2019. Atrial fibrillation on warfarin. Gout. Mild depression treated with sertraline.

Medications on admission: metoprolol 25 mg twice daily, lisinopril 10 mg daily, metformin 500 mg twice daily, warfarin 5 mg daily, atorvastatin 40 mg at night and allopurinol 100 mg daily.

Physical examination: Temperature 37.8 C, heart rate 104, blood pressure 148/92, respiratory rate 24, oxygen saturation 89% on room air. Crackles were heard at both lung bases. There was 2+ pitting edema of the lower extremities. The abdomen was soft and non-tender.

Laboratory data: White blood cell count 13.2, hemoglobin 10.9, platelets 210. Sodium 134, potassium 5.1, creatinine 2.1 (baseline 1.6). BNP was elevated at 1,480. Troponin was negative twice. INR 2.7.

Imaging: A chest x-ray showed bilateral pleural effusions and pulmonary vascular congestion, with a possible infiltrate in the right lower lobe. An echocardiogram showed an ejection fraction of 35%, reduced from 50% last year.

Assessment and plan: Acute on chronic systolic heart failure, likely precipitated by community-acquired pneumonia. We started intravenous furosemide 40 mg twice daily and ceftriaxone with azithromycin. Metformin was held because of the acute kidney injury. Repeat basic metabolic panel in the morning. Continue warfarin with daily INR checks. Consult cardiology for evaluation of the new drop in ejection fraction.

Hospital course: The dyspnea improved after diuresis, and he was weaned to 2 liters of oxygen by nasal cannula on day three. Blood cultures remained negative. Creatinine returned to 1.7. He was discharged home on oral furosemide 40 mg daily with instructions to weigh himself every morning and to follow up with his primary care physician in one week.
